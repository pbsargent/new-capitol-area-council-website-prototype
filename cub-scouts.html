Welcome to the pack

What is Cub Scouts?

Cub Scouts is a year-round program for children in kindergarten through fifth grade. Children meet in small, grade-based groups called dens. Several dens and their families make up a pack.

How often do Cub Scouts meet? Dens commonly meet two or three times a month, while the pack gathers for larger activities such as service projects, camping, and the Pinewood Derby. Each pack sets its own calendar.

What families can expect

  • Hands-on fun: games, projects, outdoor skills, and age-appropriate challenges
  • Shared experiences: parents and caregivers participate alongside their children
  • Simple progress: Scouts complete adventures and earn a badge for their grade
  • Local community: families join a nearby pack led by trained volunteers

New family questions

Know before your first meeting.

Do parents participate?

Yes. Cub Scouting is family-centered and runs through volunteer leadership. Parents and caregivers help with activities, meetings, transportation, and small pack jobs. Lion and Tiger Scouts participate with an adult partner.

What does it cost?

Families pay a national membership fee, and packs may charge local dues for their program. Activity and camp fees vary. Ask the pack for its current total before joining; the council’s Cub Scout Basics page explains costs, fundraising, and available assistance.

Do we need camping experience?

No. Cub Scout camping is designed for families, usually lasts no longer than a weekend, and focuses on having a safe, enjoyable outdoor experience together.

What should we do first?

Find nearby packs, contact a leader, and visit a meeting or activity. Packs share the same Cub Scout program, but meeting times, traditions, costs, and calendars differ—choose the fit that works for your family.
