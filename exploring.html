Career discovery that gets real

Your first look at the work behind the job.

Exploring is a career-education program created with businesses, public agencies, schools, and community organizations. Instead of only reading about a career, you meet the people doing it and learn in the places where it happens.

Who can join? Capitol Area Council serves young women and men in grades 6–12 and up to age 20. Middle school groups are called Clubs; high school and older groups are called Posts.

Exploring at a glance

  • Do the work: practice skills through projects, scenarios, demonstrations, tours, and service
  • Meet the people: learn from professionals who can answer the questions a job description cannot
  • Find your direction: confirm an interest, discover a new one, or confidently rule out a path

What happens once you join

Go from curious to capable.

Every post is different because every career is different. What they share is a learn-by-doing approach.

01

Step inside

Visit real workplaces and see the tools, standards, and teamwork behind the job.

02

Try the skills

Learn through demonstrations, scenarios, projects, training, and service.

03

Ask the experts

Talk with professionals about education, daily work, workplace culture, and career paths.

04

Lead the team

Build communication, confidence, responsibility, and professional habits.

One program, two age-appropriate formats

Clubs introduce. Posts go deeper.

Both formats combine activities and mentorship. The difference is the age group and depth of the experience.

Common questions

Your next step can be small.

Do I need to know which career I want?

No. Exploring is meant to help you discover possibilities. Trying a field can confirm an interest, reveal a new one, or show you that another direction fits better.

Can I visit before joining?

Many programs welcome prospective members, but practices vary. Use the program link or the current council directory to contact the advisor before attending.

What should I expect at a meeting?

Activities vary by field and may include facility tours, skill practice, projects, scenarios, guest speakers, team challenges, and community service.

What if my career interest is not listed?

Local opportunities change as new partners join. Browse national Exploring career fields, then contact Capitol Area Council about current or developing programs.

Ready to look behind the scenes?

Choose a field. Make one connection.

Open the current directory, find the opportunity that interests you, and contact the listed advisor to ask about a visit.
